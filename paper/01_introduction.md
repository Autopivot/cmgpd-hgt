# 1 Introduction

China's imperial-era genealogical record is, on its face, an extraordinary demographic archive. The Qing genealogies aggregated under the China Multi-Generational Panel Dataset — Liaoning (CMGPD-LN, 1749–1909) encode 62 person-level attributes across 1.51 M observations covering six generations of bannermen households [cmgpd-ln-2010]. Yet a structural asymmetry pervades the archive and every *jiapu* drawn from it: women appear only as *daughters* of recorded fathers, and their lifelines terminate at the natal lineage. A wife's record, if it exists at all, is typically reduced to a single surname character. Edges between marriage partners ($r_{hw}$) and edges from a child to their mother ($r_{md}, r_{ms}$) are therefore either absent, surnames-only, or coarsely imputed — precisely the substrate one needs to reconstruct the *maternal kinship network* that historians have long argued mediated political coalitions, household labour allocation, and the flow of marriage-market resources across banners [wang2007muxia, mann2002precious].

Reconstructing those missing edges from sparse, lopsided evidence is the methodological gap this paper addresses. We treat the problem as a constrained link-prediction task on a heterogeneous social graph, training under a deliberate ablation that mirrors the jiapu record gap: all marriage and maternal edges are removed before learning, and the trained model is asked to recover them. A heterogeneous graph transformer [hu2020hgt] together with an SEAL-based subgraph-pattern matcher [zhang2018seal] provides a strong distributional baseline. But two limitations motivate **[SYSTEM]**. First, even a well-tuned HGT exhibits a familiar ranking-vs-decision gap on this panel: the true wife appears within the top-10 candidates with high recall (Hit@10 = `XXX`, MRR = `XXX`), but Hit@1 stays well below the rank-and-commit threshold a historian would trust (`XXX`). Second, the model is opaque; a probability score offers no narrative purchase to a domain expert, and offers no surface for injecting the *partial* archival evidence (a surviving surname, a banner constraint, an in-marriage event recorded in DS0003) that genealogists routinely have but cannot encode in a feature vector.

[SYSTEM] is our answer to both gaps. It is a six-panel visual analytics workspace built on top of the HGT+SEAL backbone, designed around a single premise: *a marriage edge worth committing to a reconstructed genealogy must be defensible by either a graph pattern, a deliberative narrative, or both, and never by a black-box score alone*. To this end [SYSTEM] couples the GNN ranking with an LLM multi-agent negotiation in which each marriage-age individual is enrolled as an autonomous persona grounded in their CMGPD-DS0003 life events and estimated income trajectory; a six-round one-to-many bilateral protocol re-prices the HGT shortlist under round-by-round expert hints; and an audit log preserves every commit with a multi-label provenance tag (HGT, MAS, or both).

We summarise our contributions:

- **A visual analytics workspace**, [SYSTEM], that pairs a per-cohort honeycomb embedding view with an LLM-driven negotiation arena, bridging GNN link prediction and historian-in-the-loop reasoning over a single shared cohort context.
- **An ablation-mirrored evaluation harness** that simulates the jiapu record gap directly on CMGPD-LN by removing every $r_{hw}$ and $r_{md}/r_{ms}$ edge prior to training, allowing us to measure reconstruction quality against ground truth.
- **A six-round bilateral negotiation protocol** whose final ranking obeys $\mathrm{score}_i = \tfrac{1}{2}(s_i + t_i) - \lambda\,|s_i - t_i|$, rewarding pairs whose mutual high regard is symmetric, with hint injection drained between rounds for human-in-the-loop steering.
- **A multi-label provenance scheme** on every committed edge that distinguishes HGT-batch acceptances from MAS-overrides and flags the convergent case where both pipelines agree, supporting cumulative-error diagnosis through a one-click restore.
