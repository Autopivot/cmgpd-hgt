# 1 Introduction

Imperial-era Chinese family genealogies — *jiapu* (家谱) — are the single largest surviving record of late-imperial kinship, but they are systematically lopsided: by patrilineal convention they document male descendants in detail while reducing wives and daughters to a surname character or omitting them outright. Edges between marriage partners ($r_{hw}$) and edges from a child to their mother ($r_{md}, r_{ms}$) are therefore either absent, surnames-only, or coarsely imputed across the *jiapu* corpus — precisely the substrate one would need to reconstruct the *maternal kinship network* that historians have long argued mediated political coalitions, household labour allocation, and the flow of marriage-market resources across banners [wang2007muxia, mann2002precious]. The methodological gap this paper addresses is how to fill those missing edges in a way a historian can defend, given that no jiapu corpus carries its own ground truth. Our solution is to use a separate Qing-era resource — the China Multi-Generational Panel Dataset — Liaoning (CMGPD-LN, 1749–1909) [cmgpd-ln-2010], a demographic panel compiled from triennial population registers maintained by the Banner administration in Liaoning — as an evaluation testbed. CMGPD-LN encodes 62 person-level attributes across 1.51 M observations covering six generations of bannermen households and, crucially, *does* preserve the marriage and maternal edges that jiapu suppress. We use CMGPD-LN to **simulate** the jiapu completion task: ablate its $r_{hw}$ and $r_{md}/r_{ms}$ edges to mimic the jiapu record gap, train on the ablated graph, and grade reconstructions against the held-out edges that the panel — but not any real jiapu — preserves. The deployment target is real jiapu; CMGPD-LN provides the methodology validation that jiapu themselves cannot.

Concretely, we treat reconstruction as a constrained link-prediction task on a heterogeneous social graph, training under a deliberate ablation of the testbed that mirrors the jiapu record gap: all marriage and maternal edges are removed from the CMGPD-LN graph before learning, and the trained model is asked to recover them. A heterogeneous graph transformer [hu2020hgt] together with an SEAL-based subgraph-pattern matcher [zhang2018seal] provides a strong distributional baseline. But two limitations motivate **[SYSTEM]**. First, even a well-tuned HGT exhibits a familiar ranking-vs-decision gap on this panel: across 131k held-out pairs the true wife appears within the top-10 candidates in 62.4% of cases (Hit@10 = 0.624, MRR = 0.428) and the model is well-calibrated (ROC-AUC = 0.99, PR-AUC = 0.95); per-cohort Hungarian assignment recovers 62.6% of marriages — yet *unconstrained* Hit@1 stays at 32.5%, well below the rank-and-commit threshold a historian would trust. Pushed harder, an ablation comparison reveals that maternal edges contribute ≈0 marginal predictability above marriage edges (Δ Hungarian recall@1 = −0.047 against the unablated upper bound), confirming that the residual error is not absorbed by the graph alone. Second, the model is opaque; a probability score offers no narrative purchase to a domain expert, and offers no surface for injecting the *partial* archival evidence (a surviving surname, a banner constraint, an in-marriage event recorded in DS0003) that genealogists working with real jiapu routinely have but cannot encode in a feature vector.

[SYSTEM] is our answer to both gaps. It is a six-panel visual analytics workspace built on top of the HGT+SEAL backbone, designed around a single premise: *a marriage edge worth committing to a reconstructed jiapu must be defensible by either a graph pattern, a deliberative narrative, or both, and never by a black-box score alone*. To this end [SYSTEM] couples the GNN ranking with an LLM multi-agent negotiation in which each marriage-age individual on the CMGPD-LN testbed is enrolled as an autonomous persona grounded in their CMGPD-DS0003 life events and estimated income trajectory; a six-round one-to-many bilateral protocol re-prices the HGT shortlist under round-by-round expert hints; and an audit log preserves every commit with a multi-label provenance tag (HGT, MAS, or both).

We summarise our contributions:

- **A visual analytics workspace**, [SYSTEM], that pairs a per-cohort honeycomb embedding view with an LLM-driven negotiation arena, bridging GNN link prediction on the CMGPD-LN testbed and historian-in-the-loop reasoning over a single shared cohort context, with the deployment target being real jiapu reconstruction.
- **An ablation-mirrored evaluation harness** that simulates the jiapu record gap directly on the CMGPD-LN demographic panel by removing every $r_{hw}$ and $r_{md}/r_{ms}$ edge prior to training, allowing us to measure reconstruction quality against the panel's preserved ground truth — a benchmark that no jiapu corpus can supply on its own.
- **A six-round bilateral negotiation protocol** whose final ranking obeys $\mathrm{score}_i = \tfrac{1}{2}(s_i + t_i) - \lambda\,|s_i - t_i|$, rewarding pairs whose mutual high regard is symmetric, with hint injection drained between rounds for human-in-the-loop steering.
- **A multi-label provenance scheme** on every committed edge that distinguishes HGT-batch acceptances from MAS-overrides and flags the convergent case where both pipelines agree, supporting cumulative-error diagnosis through a one-click restore.
