# Abstract

Imperial-era Chinese family genealogies (*jiapu*, 家谱) preserve detailed records of male descent yet systematically erase women: a wife's identity rarely exceeds her surname, and a daughter's record terminates at her natal lineage. The structural absence of marriage edges in jiapu obscures the maternal kinship network — the *muxia* (母家, "mother's house") — whose role in late-imperial politics is well documented but quantitatively under-studied. We introduce **[SYSTEM]**, a visual analytics workspace that treats genealogical completion as an *interpretable link-prediction* task in which a heterogeneous graph transformer (HGT) proposes candidate marriages and an LLM-driven multi-agent negotiation re-litigates each proposal under historian-injectable constraints. Because no jiapu corpus carries its own ground truth, we use the CMGPD-LN demographic panel — a separate Qing resource compiled from triennial Banner population registers in Liaoning, which preserves marriage and maternal edges that jiapu suppress — as an evaluation testbed: we ablate its $r_{hw}$ and $r_{md}/r_{ms}$ edges to simulate the jiapu-style record gap, then grade reconstruction against the held-out edges. On this testbed the HGT layer alone reaches Hit@10 = `XXX` and MRR = `XXX` but only Hit@1 = `XXX`; [SYSTEM] closes the residual gap by routing low-confidence cases through a six-round bilateral persona-grounded negotiation, raising the operator's acceptance precision to `XXX` on a held-out 1882 cohort while preserving an audit trail that aligns each commit with one or more SEAL motifs and (when available) sparse archival hints. Three case studies with a demographer, a genealogist, and a Qing political historian demonstrate that [SYSTEM] converts an opaque GNN ranking into a deliberative workflow whose every accepted edge is justified by either a pattern in the graph, a story in the agents' transcript, or both — a workflow the validation testbed certifies and the jiapu deployment target requires.
